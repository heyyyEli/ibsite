Language A Language and Literature HL/SL

Course Overview

This course focuses on the study of literary and non‑literary texts in the student’s best language. Students develop skills in analysis, interpretation, and critical thinking while exploring a wide range of genres and global perspectives.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Fewer texts and slightly shorter assessments, with focus on core analytical skills.
  • HL: More texts, deeper literary analysis, and an additional HL essay component.

Career Pathways

Law, journalism, media, education, publishing, communications, and any field requiring strong reading and writing skills.

Persian A

Course Overview

Persian A develops advanced reading, writing, and analytical skills in Persian through the study of literature and other texts. Students explore cultural identity, themes, and stylistic features in Persian‑language works.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Focus on core texts and fundamental literary analysis.
  • HL: More texts, deeper critical engagement, and extended written work.

Career Pathways

Translation, diplomacy, journalism, teaching, cultural studies, and roles involving Persian language and literature.

Language B HL/SL

Course Overview

Language B is for students with prior experience in the language. It develops communication skills in reading, writing, listening, and speaking, while exploring cultural topics and real‑world contexts.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Everyday communication, basic texts, and practical language use.
  • HL: More complex texts, deeper cultural analysis, and extended writing tasks.

Career Pathways

International relations, tourism, translation, global business, diplomacy, and education.

Language ab initio SL

Course Overview

Language ab initio is designed for complete beginners. Students build foundational vocabulary and grammar to communicate in everyday situations and gain basic cultural awareness.

HL vs SL

  • SL only: Ab initio is offered only at SL, with a focus on practical communication.

Career Pathways

Tourism, hospitality, international communication, and roles where basic language skills are an advantage.

Digital Society HL/SL

Course Overview

Digital Society explores how digital technologies shape individuals, communities, and global systems. Students examine topics like social media, AI, data privacy, digital ethics, and online culture.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core concepts of digital technologies and their social impact.
  • HL: Deeper investigations, extended projects, and more complex case studies.

Career Pathways

Digital media, UX design, tech policy, communications, cybersecurity, and social research.

Business HL/SL

Course Overview

Business Management examines how organizations operate through marketing, finance, human resources, and strategy. Students analyze real‑world case studies and learn how decisions affect different stakeholders.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core business concepts and basic analytical tools.
  • HL: More quantitative analysis, deeper strategic thinking, and extended case work.

Career Pathways

Business administration, marketing, entrepreneurship, finance, management consulting, and international business.

Psychology HL/SL

Course Overview

Psychology explores human behavior and mental processes through biological, cognitive, and sociocultural approaches. Students study key theories, research methods, and real‑world applications.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core approaches and one optional topic.
  • HL: Additional research methods, more content depth, and extended analysis.

Career Pathways

Clinical psychology, counseling, neuroscience, education, social work, and human resources.

Biology HL/SL

Course Overview

Biology studies living organisms, from cells and genetics to ecosystems and human physiology. Students develop scientific inquiry skills through experiments and data analysis.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core biological concepts and standard lab work.
  • HL: Greater depth in genetics, metabolism, and molecular biology.

Career Pathways

Medicine, biotechnology, genetics, environmental science, research, and healthcare professions.

Chemistry HL/SL

Course Overview

Chemistry examines the composition and behavior of matter, including atomic structure, bonding, energetics, kinetics, and organic chemistry. Practical lab work is a key component.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core chemical principles and standard calculations.
  • HL: More complex mechanisms, advanced energetics, and deeper organic chemistry.

Career Pathways

Medicine, pharmacy, chemical engineering, biochemistry, environmental science, and materials science.

Physics HL/SL

Course Overview

Physics explores motion, forces, energy, waves, electricity, and modern physics. Students apply mathematics to understand and model physical phenomena.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core topics with standard mathematical depth.
  • HL: Additional topics such as quantum physics and relativity, with more advanced math.

Career Pathways

Engineering, astrophysics, robotics, aviation, renewable energy, and scientific research.

Computer Science HL/SL

Course Overview

Computer Science develops computational thinking, programming skills, and understanding of algorithms, data structures, and system design. Students also consider ethical and social implications of technology.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Programming fundamentals, basic algorithms, and core theory.
  • HL: Advanced algorithms, object‑oriented design, and deeper theoretical content.

Career Pathways

Software engineering, AI, cybersecurity, data science, game development, and IT systems.

Mathematics Analysis and Approaches HL/SL

Course Overview

Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches focuses on algebra, functions, calculus, and mathematical reasoning. It is suited for students who enjoy abstract thinking and rigorous problem‑solving.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Core calculus, algebra, and functions.
  • HL: More advanced calculus, proofs, and deeper theoretical concepts.

Career Pathways

Engineering, physics, computer science, economics, mathematics, and quantitative finance.

Visual Arts HL/SL

Course Overview

Visual Arts encourages creative exploration through drawing, painting, sculpture, digital media, and mixed techniques. Students develop a personal artistic voice while studying global art traditions.

HL vs SL

  • SL: Focus on studio work and basic art analysis with a smaller portfolio.
  • HL: More extensive portfolio, deeper experimentation, and a more detailed comparative study.

Career Pathways

Graphic design, architecture, animation, fine arts, illustration, fashion design, and creative media fields.
